How to get a client to sign off on changes

Published 2026-11-02 · SignedChange Guides

Most freelancers and contractors know they should get changes signed. The hard part is asking without sounding like you don't trust the client, and getting a busy person to actually sign. Here's how to make sign-off a smooth, normal part of every project.

Set the expectation at the start

The best time to talk about changes is before any happen. At kickoff, say: "Whenever something changes, I'll send you a one-minute note to confirm it. It keeps us both clear on price and timing." When the first change comes, the client already expects it. Add a short clause to your agreement too; see our scope creep clause examples.

Frame it as protecting both sides

Clients don't like feeling accused. Signed changes protect them too: they know the exact price before the work starts, and they can prove what you promised. Say so. "This way there are no surprises on the invoice" lands much better than "I need this in case you don't pay".

Ask immediately, not later

Send the change for signature while the conversation is fresh, ideally within minutes. A day later, the client is busy and the details are fuzzy. A week later, you've already done the work and lost your leverage.

Make it short and specific

A one-page change note gets signed. A five-page document gets "I'll look at it this weekend". Include only: what changes, the price impact, the timing impact and the signatures. A change order template keeps it consistent.

Make signing effortless

Remove every obstacle. No printing, no scanning, no app to install, no account to create. A link they can open on their phone from WhatsApp or email works best. With SignedChange, you fill in the change, tap share, and they sign with a finger on their own phone. Both sides then download the same PDF.

If you're with the client in person, hand them your phone and get the signature on the spot.

Don't start until it's signed

This is the rule that makes everything else work. Politely: "I'll start as soon as it's signed." Most clients sign within minutes when the work they want is waiting on it. If someone repeatedly avoids signing, treat it as a warning sign about payment.

Handle pushback calmly

  • "Can't we just trust each other?" — "Of course, this just makes sure we remember the same thing."
  • "It's such a small change." — "Absolutely, which is why the note takes 30 seconds."
  • "Just add it to the invoice." — "Happy to; I just need your OK on the price first so there are no surprises."

A ready-to-send message

Here's a message you can adapt and send with your signing link:

Hi [name], as discussed, here's a quick note confirming the change: [short description], +$[amount], new delivery [date]. Please tap the link to review and sign; it takes under a minute on your phone. I'll start as soon as it's signed. Thanks! [link]

It's short, friendly, confirms what you discussed, and makes the next step obvious. Sending the same style of message every time also trains clients to expect it, so sign-off becomes routine rather than a negotiation.

If you work with a team or agency, decide who is allowed to approve changes on the client's side and who sends change notes on yours. Clear roles prevent changes being agreed in side conversations that never reach the invoice.

Keep a clean record

Number each change, keep the signed copies together, and reference them on your invoices. When the project ends, the full history is in one place. If a dispute happens anyway, you're prepared; see freelance payment dispute.

Frequently asked questions

What if the client approves by email but won't sign?

An email approval is useful evidence. Keep it. A signature is stronger, so keep asking politely for future changes.

Should I charge for small changes?

That's your decision. Even free changes should be recorded so the scope stays clear.

How fast should a client sign?

Ideally before any extra work starts. With a phone-friendly link, most sign the same day.

Can I get a signature from a client in another country?

Yes, a remote signing link works anywhere with internet. Check local rules for high-value contracts.
